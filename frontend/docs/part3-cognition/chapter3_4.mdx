# Chapter 3: Nav2 - Path Planning for Bipedal Humanoid Movement

## Introduction

Navigation2 (Nav2) represents the state-of-the-art navigation framework for ROS 2, providing sophisticated path planning and execution capabilities for mobile robots. For bipedal humanoid robots, Nav2 requires specialized adaptations to account for the unique kinematic constraints, balance requirements, and locomotion patterns that distinguish legged locomotion from wheeled or tracked systems. This chapter explores the implementation of Nav2 for humanoid robots, addressing the specific challenges of bipedal navigation and the integration of human-like movement patterns.

## Explanation

Traditional Nav2 implementations assume wheeled or holonomic robot platforms with relatively simple kinematic constraints. Bipedal humanoid robots present unique challenges including limited turning radius, balance constraints, step-based locomotion, and the need for dynamic balance control. These requirements necessitate modifications to standard path planning algorithms, costmap representations, and local planning strategies to generate trajectories that the humanoid robot can safely execute while maintaining stability.

### Bipedal Navigation Challenges

Humanoid robots face specific navigation challenges:
- **Balance Constraints**: Maintaining center of mass within support polygon
- **Step-Based Locomotion**: Discrete foot placements rather than continuous motion
- **Limited Mobility**: Restricted turning and lateral movement compared to wheeled robots
- **Dynamic Stability**: Requirement for continuous motion to maintain balance
- **Footstep Planning**: Coordination of foot placement with path following

### Nav2 Architecture for Humanoids

Adapting Nav2 for humanoid platforms requires modifications to:
- **Costmap Representation**: Integration of terrain traversability for bipedal locomotion
- **Global Planner**: Path optimization considering humanoid kinematic constraints
- **Local Planner**: Dynamic trajectory generation for stable bipedal motion
- **Controller Server**: Gait-specific control algorithms for legged locomotion
- **Recovery Behaviors**: Humanoid-specific recovery actions for navigation failures

### Footstep Planning Integration

Specialized planning considerations include:
- **Terrain Analysis**: Assessment of ground stability and obstacle clearance
- **Footstep Sequences**: Generation of stable foot placement patterns
- **ZMP Control**: Zero Moment Point maintenance during walking
- **Dynamic Balance**: Integration with balance controllers during navigation

### Humanoid-Specific Navigation Components

Key adaptations include:
- **Bipedal Controllers**: Gait generators for stable walking patterns
- **Balance Monitors**: Real-time stability assessment during navigation
- **Step Adjustment**: Dynamic modification of footstep plans based on terrain
- **Recovery Actions**: Humanoid-specific behaviors for obstacle avoidance
